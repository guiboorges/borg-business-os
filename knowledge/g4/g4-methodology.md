# Report: G4 Educação / G4 (Gestão 4.0) Knowledge Base

## 1. Summary of G4 and its pillars

**G4** (founded in 2019 as "Gestão 4.0" — "Management 4.0" — later "G4 Educação", today operating at g4business.com) is Brazil's largest school of business management and company scaling. Founders: **Tallis Gomes** (Easy Taxi, Singu, author of "Nada Easy"), **Alfredo Soares** (Xtech Commerce, sold to VTEX; author of "Bora Vender", "Bora Varejo" and "Todos Somos uma Marca"; chairman of Loja Integrada) and **Bruno Nardon** (Kanui, former president of Rappi Brazil, partner at the Norte fund). Current CEO: **Misa Antonini**.

**Institutional numbers (/sobre page):** went from R$ 11M to **R$ 508M in revenue in 2025** (average growth of ~90% a year), with no outside investors; **87k+ companies impacted**; **BHAG: create 1 million jobs** (1.09M already reported) and R$ 1B in revenue by 2027.

**The house's core thesis:** "real growth is built with method, not luck" — taught by operators who have already scaled companies ("people who have done it before"), not by theorists. The summary concept is **traction**: "a company's ability to grow predictably and systematically, without depending on the founder's individual effort" — processes that work, autonomous teams and indicators that guide decisions.

**Recurring pillars in everything G4 teaches:**
1. **Method over intuition** — data-driven decisions, management rituals with a cadence.
2. **Predictability** — structured funnel + metrics = predictable revenue.
3. **Scalable founder** — making the company independent of the owner.
4. **Strong culture** as the backbone of strategy ("the way we do things around here").
5. **Business maths** — unit economics (CAC, LTV, margin, payback) as the compass for scaling.

## 3. Frameworks and methodologies (practical explanation for application)

### 3.1 The G4 sales funnel (the core framework of the "sales machine")
- **Simple version (3 stages)** for smaller businesses: (1) **Demand generation** — define what a potential buyer is; (2) **Opportunity** — criteria that select those with a real ability to buy; (3) **Sale** — what makes these people pay.
- **Extended version (9 stages)** for mature operations: Awareness/prospecting → Discovery → Qualification (MQL) → Evaluation → Presentation → Intent/negotiation → Closing → Onboarding → Loyalty (upsell/cross-sell).
- **Golden rules:** measure the whole funnel, not one isolated stage; compare yourself with your own history month by month (not with the market); know **how much a lead costs at each stage**; have SLAs between marketing and sales; the classic top-of-funnel mistake = no SLA defining the expected result.
- **Qualification:** MQL (marketing qualified) → **SAL** (Sales Accepted Lead — an intermediate stage used by G4 itself: the salesperson accepts the lead as legitimate, which measures the health of the marketing↔sales SLA) → SQL (sales qualified, with fit + interest + ICP match) → customer. Use **lead scoring** based on the patterns of customers who closed.
- **Step by step to build it:** map the customer journey → define stages and milestones → define MQL/SQL criteria with marketing and sales together → implement lead scoring → optimise conversions stage by stage (visitor→lead, lead→MQL, MQL→customer), cycle time and cost per sale. Manage everything in a CRM.
- **Variations taught:** **Y funnel** (active prospecting: prospect-connect-qualify-nurture-sell + passive: convert-engage — the most complete form), **AIDA** (Attention-Interest-Desire-Action), **AARRR Pirate Funnel**, a circular acquisition model (non-linear buying with repeat purchases). Differences between B2B (long cycle, multiple decision-makers, dense content) vs B2C (volume, promotions) vs e-commerce (SEO/ads → cart → optimise checkout against abandonment).

### 3.2 Pirate Funnel / Growth (AARRR — Dave McClure, adapted by G4)
Acquisition (PPC paid traffic + organic/SEO) → Activation (first meaningful action: email marketing, chatbot, rich content, landing pages, trial — the lead becomes an MQL) → Retention (map reasons for drop-off, form hypotheses, validate with A/B tests) → Referral (referral programs = low-cost growth, "zero CAC") → Revenue. Goal: find **bottlenecks** and continuously optimise the journey.

### 3.3 Growth Hacking (Bruno Nardon is the in-house reference)
A mindset that applies to the whole company, not just marketing. A cyclical process: **data analysis/insights → idea generation → experiment prioritisation → running the experiments**. Principles: constant testing, funnel optimisation, automation/scalability, data-driven decisions. Growth comes from the sum of small compounding gains, not from a "silver bullet". G4's own growth tripod: **content + community + software**.

### 3.4 OKR (the house's goal-setting methodology)
- Structure in 3 levels: **Objective** (qualitative, inspiring) + **Key Results** (verifiable metrics, scored from 0 to 1) + **Initiatives** (actions that move the KRs).
- **4 pillars:** (1) *Less is more* — 3 to 5 OKRs per cycle, no "weights"; (2) *Aggressive goals produce better results* — by the Google standard, 70% achievement is already excellent for ambitious goals; (3) *Co-created goals engage people* — ~50% of KRs defined by the people who execute them, all negotiated with the manager, and all OKRs visible to the whole company (no hierarchical cascade); (4) *Flexibility* — quarterly cycles, KRs can be adjusted mid-cycle; **don't tie them to bonuses**.
- **Two types:** *committed* OKRs (operational, require 1.0) and *aspirational* ones (moonshots, 0.7 is great). The mix depends on the company's moment.
- **Cycle review in 3 parts:** an objective score (average of the KRs, 0–1; Google scale: 0.7–1.0 green, 0.4–0.6 yellow, 0–0.3 red), a subjective self-assessment (context can raise or lower the score) and reflection/learning.
- **Rituals:** weekly check-ins, monthly indicator review, quarterly close with learnings.
- **OKR vs KPI:** "KPIs measure the operation (how we're doing); OKRs drive change (where we want to get to)". Good KPIs are relevant, reliable, comparable and actionable, and always have an owner. Types: strategic (revenue, NPS), tactical (conversion, CAC), operational (service time).
- **SMART goals** as the writing criterion for KRs: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. Template: "our goal is [quantifiable objective] by [deadline]; we will achieve it through [steps]; this helps the company with [benefit]".

### 3.5 Management by Results
Three pillars: **clear objectives + consistent indicators + follow-up rituals** (meetings with an agenda, an owner and a cadence). It addresses three pains: strategy-operations misalignment, difficulty measuring, and too many priorities.

### 3.6 High-performance management (based on Andy Grove / "High Output Management")
- A manager must identify the **limiting step** in the process (the "egg step" in the breakfast example) and optimise resources around it.
- **Choose ~5 daily indicators** (sales forecast, inventory, equipment condition, workforce, perceived quality), **pair indicators with results** (e.g. calls made × deals closed) and analyse trends.
- A manager's success = the team's output. Meetings are a management tool: mission-oriented (one-off decisions) and process-oriented (recurring) — with emphasis on a **weekly ~1h 1:1** with direct reports.
- Low performance = lack of skill OR of motivation (test: "if their life depended on it, could they do it?").
- Rituals G4 puts in place in companies: **All Hands, S&OP, Revenue Committee, 1:1** — each ritual with an owner, an agenda and a cadence.

### 3.7 ICP, lead types and prospecting
- **ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)** ≠ target audience ≠ persona: it's an almost strictly sales concept — grouping leads likely to convert so you find, attract, approach and negotiate only with those who can close. Gather: interaction with the brand, decision-makers, demographics, pains/dreams, value opportunities. It delivers focus, personalisation (86% buy more from tailored offers), the right channels and higher conversion. It is the #1 lever for lowering CAC.
- **Seeds, Nets and Spears** (3 lead sources): *Seeds* = referral/word of mouth (highest conversion, lowest cost), *Nets* = inbound and mass media (volume; segment carefully "where you cast the net"), *Spears* = outbound/active prospecting with SDRs (precision, requires a prepared team; doesn't make sense for low tickets). Choose the mix by average ticket and model (B2B/B2C), master one channel before diversifying, test a lot and measure the jumps between funnel stages.
- **Inside Sales / SDR:** the SDR qualifies and prepares the lead ("makes the assist for the goal"); build the relationship before the offer.
- **Marketing↔sales SLA:** define the characteristics of an accepted lead (e.g. "every MQL must be in the ICP"), minimum/maximum MQL volumes, campaign visibility for sales, seasonality forecasts. Alternative: "Smarketing" (both areas under the same umbrella).
- Sales techniques taught: **SPIN Selling**, negotiation frameworks (used by Ambev/Amazon), applied neuroscience, Ask Method, social selling and personal branding (Alfredo).

### 3.8 Organisational culture
"Culture is how we do things around here" — there is no right/wrong culture, only **strong or weak**; it must be coherent. Practical functions: enables autonomous decisions in situations no playbook foresaw (VUCA world), aligns the modus operandi as the team grows, attracts talent (88% of candidates consider culture). It connects to hiring: cultural fit as a criterion, strategic people management tied to goals. In G4 Gestão e Estratégia: a performance culture that is "desirable, credible and profitable" + a review of the "promise to talent". Tallis: a growth culture is the main trait of exponential companies; "always be interviewing" (at Easy Taxi, recruiters spent ¼ of their time interviewing).

### 3.9 Value ladder (offer strategy)
Products that connect in steps of increasing value: a customer who buys the 1st product is more likely to buy the next ones → **it lowers CAC (repeat purchases with no acquisition cost) and raises LTV at the same time**. It is G4's own model (free content → online programs → immersions → memberships).

## 4. Business maths: the metrics and formulas G4 preaches

**Core formulas:**
- **CAC** = (total marketing + sales spend) ÷ number of new customers in the period. Include: media/ads, **team salaries**, tools/tech, content production costs, inventory. Common mistake: counting only media spend (artificially low CAC).
- **ARPU** = average ticket × purchase frequency in the period.
- **LTV** = ARPU × Gross Margin (contribution margin %). Simple version: average ticket × frequency × customer lifetime.
- **Acquisition ROI** = LTV ÷ CAC. Ranges (Dener Lippert, adopted by G4): **>5:1 hypergrowth; 3–5:1 sustainable growth; 1–3:1 growth; <1:1 unsustainable**. Standard benchmark: **LTV ≥ 3× CAC**; above 5–6× may indicate under-investment in acquisition. Key insight: lowering CAC isn't always the answer — it can be worth increasing spend and accepting a lower ROI if absolute net profit grows.
- **CAC payback**: in recurring revenue, CAC should pay for itself within **12 months** of the customer's revenue.
- **Churn Rate** = (customers lost ÷ active customers at the start) × 100. SMBs: 3–7%/month is common (= 36–76% of the base per year). A double cost: lost revenue + replacement cost (acquiring costs 5–25× more than retaining; +5% retention = +25–95% profit — Bain). The concept of **invisible churn**: 25 out of 26 unhappy customers leave without complaining; pre-churn signals to monitor: falling usage/purchase frequency, silence after a problem, downgrades, not answering surveys, "praise without action", a change of decision-maker. Practical diagnosis: list last year's 30 biggest customers and mark who is still active and when the last proactive contact happened.
- **MRR** = number of customers × average monthly value; the basis of predictability. **NRR** (Net Revenue Retention): >100% = the base expands via upsell even with cancellations; <80% = a retention warning.
- **Magic Number** = quarterly ΔMRR ÷ the quarter's marketing+sales spend. >0.75 good; 0.5–0.75 moderate; <0.5 warning. G4 treats it as a "useful hack" when LTV/CAC isn't available — it ignores margin and retention.
- **Unit economics** = value generated per unit (seat, ride, customer). Model 1 (per item): **contribution margin = price − variable costs**; any transaction with a margin >0 contributes to fixed costs. **Unit margin** = contribution margin ÷ transactions. Model 2 (per customer): **UE = LTV ÷ CAC**. It prevents the "leaky bucket" company (high CAC + low LTV).
- **OTE (On Target Earnings)**: base + commission at 100% of target — aligns the sales team's pay and targets.
- **Financial indicators every company should track** (the "11 indicators" list): CAC, fixed costs, variable costs, **EBITDA** (= net operating profit + depreciation + amortisation), gross margin, net margin, average ticket, LTV, cash flow, break-even point, ROI. + the **P&L** as an "X-ray" against the "high revenue, low profit" trap; receivables/inventory/payables periods in the financial routine (Traction). Margin realism: the real average margin of companies is ~7–8% (NYU Stern), not the 36% the public imagines.
- **The funnel as a forecasting machine:** conversion rates per stage × history = revenue projection (G4 hands out a "Sales Predictability Spreadsheet" to project results from historical data). Cited data: 68% of companies don't measure their funnel; 79% of marketing leads never become sales.
- Mantra: **"what gets measured gets managed; what gets measured and fed back gets done well; what gets rewarded gets repeated"**.

## 5. Concepts by area (summary for the agents)

**Operations:** traction = growing without the founder; rituals with an owner/agenda/cadence (weekly 1:1, All Hands, S&OP, Revenue Committee); ~5 daily indicators; identify the limiting step; diagnose skill vs motivation; separate partnership roles (Owner/Investor/Board member/Manager); P&L + cash flow in the routine; PDCA, the Eisenhower matrix, squads (the Spotify model) as complementary tools.

**Goals:** quarterly OKRs (3–5 objectives, SMART KRs scored 0–1, 50% bottom-up, full transparency, no bonus attached, weekly check-in); ongoing KPIs with an owner; aggressive goals > easy goals (Locke); cycle review that is objective + subjective + reflective.

**Sales/Acquisition:** the sales machine = revenue architecture (a funnel aligned with the journey) + MQL/SAL/SQL qualification with an SLA + pipeline management (forecasting) + account management (upsell/cross-sell/repeat purchase/referral); ICP before anything else; a Seeds/Nets/Spears mix according to ticket; the SDR prepares, the closer converts; a CRM is mandatory; follow-up (don't give up early — the buying decision isn't linear); OTE for compensation; "there is only one profession: salesperson" (Alfredo).

**Marketing:** 4 pillars — inbound/attraction, relationship (CRM, email), digital (SEO, ads, automation), branding/positioning; marketing exists to become **predictable revenue**; value proposition and positioning determine CAC efficiency; content as an acquisition asset (content marketing is Alfredo's banner); the marketing funnel generates demand, the sales funnel converts — integrated by an SLA.

**Ads/Performance:** paid traffic = the Acquisition stage of AARRR (Google/Meta/LinkedIn/TikTok/YouTube); segment by ICP; evaluate a campaign by revenue generated ÷ spend (ROAS) and, more importantly, by **CAC and LTV per channel** (an expensive channel can be worth it if its LTV is higher); channel diversification as an investment portfolio; continuous A/B tests of creatives/pages; optimise the post-click funnel (landing page, checkout) before scaling spend; the Magic Number for aggregate S&M efficiency.

**Strategy:** strategic planning with a clear vision, market analysis, measurable goals, team alignment, risk management and continuous monitoring; the Growth–Control–Liquidity Triangle; a 2–3 year horizon with owners; the value ladder as the offer architecture; "one of the CEO's roles is to think of ways to destroy their own business" (Tallis); growth engines: Product-led, Sales-led, Marketing-led and **Founder-led growth**.

## 6. Public teachings of the founders

**Tallis Gomes ("Nada Easy"; 15 leadership lessons):** validate the idea before building; execution > idea; a growth culture as the exponential engine; leadership: authenticity, communication ("if you don't communicate, you don't lead"), inspire through the why, meritocracy, resilience ("being an entrepreneur is getting punched in the face 90% of the time"), humility (Dalio), empathy (Horowitz: people > product > profit), execution capacity (starting and finishing cycles), collaborative planning (Collins: the right people on the bus first), clear expectations via KPIs + 1:1s, ethics as a decision framework, calm + agility with calculated risk, adapting in crises, and "always be interviewing".

**Alfredo Soares ("Bora Vender"):** attitude is the best strategy; character + attitude; execute, fail and redo quickly; "you only get rich by creating value"; we are all salespeople and every brand is personal ("Todos Somos uma Marca" — "We Are All a Brand"); content marketing and social selling as sales channels; retail/e-commerce ("Bora Varejo"). On the blog he signs the content on sales metrics, lead types and the sales academy.

**Bruno Nardon:** the growth hacking reference; evaluate the business "end to end" to choose the growth levers; disciplined experimentation; LTV/CAC as the compass; scaled G4 with the content + community + software tripod; host of the "Papo de Gestão" podcast and co-host of "Extremos".
